# Tutoring Techniques

The AI moves that power the study loop. Each makes the *learner* think — the AI never just
delivers the answer. (Adapted from advanced prompting strategies: Socratic/self-ask,
reflection, multi-agent, iterative refinement, rubrics.)

## Socratic / Self-Ask — expose shallow understanding

Instead of explaining, ask. Probe with escalating questions until a gap appears, then let
the learner close it.
> "Before I say anything — what do you think happens when X? … Why? … What would break that?"

Good probes: *why does this exist? what problem does it solve? what's the simplest case?
where does it fail? how is it different from [nearby concept]? what would you tell a beginner?*

## Teach-back critique (Feynman + Reflection)

The learner explains from memory; you critique against **Answer → Reflect → Revise**:
- Which parts were vague, hand-wavy, or a wrong analogy?
- What did they *omit* that matters?
- Ask them to revise the explanation, then confirm it's now correct — don't hand them the
  fix; point to the gap and let them repair it.

## Quiz generation — active recall

Generate a short, mixed quiz (recall + application), one question at a time or as a set:
- **Recall:** "State the rule for …"
- **Apply:** "Given [concrete scenario], what do you do and why?"
- **Discriminate:** "When would you use A instead of B?"
Grade against what "correct" requires; name the *specific* miss, not just right/wrong. Re-ask
missed items on the next spaced review.

## Multi-agent tutor — for hard or contested topics

Simulate a short dialogue among defined roles to surface trade-offs the learner should hold:
a **practitioner** (how it's really used), a **skeptic** (where it breaks / costs), and a
**novice** (forces plain language). End by asking the learner to adjudicate.

## Rubric — grade understanding, not vibes

For a "done" check, score against explicit criteria, e.g.:
- **Correctness** — factually right, no misconceptions.
- **Completeness** — covers the core cases, not just the happy path.
- **Transfer** — can apply it to a *new* scenario, not just recite.
- **Explanation** — can teach it simply.
State the score per criterion and the one thing to improve next.

## Iterative refinement — build understanding in passes

Don't demand mastery in one pass. Draft understanding → diagnose the weakest dimension →
target just that → re-check. One dimension at a time (a concept, an edge case, an application)
beats trying to "get it all" at once.

## Depth control

Match the questioning to the target depth: *awareness* → recognition quizzes; *working* →
applied scenarios with a reference allowed; *mastery* → teach-back + novel-transfer +
"explain the trade-off" with nothing to look at.
